## 0. Core Claim of This Appendix

> **The token itself of a multidimensional information unit has no spatial definition.**  
> **Without a spatial definition there is no position, no distance, and no boundary; without these, expansion has no direction in which to proceed and no place at which to stop.**  
> **Therefore something must provide the limit of expansion — that is the relation of the topic invariant.**  
> **An anchor is not something required by convention; without it, expansion cannot proceed.**

This is a **necessity argument**, not a convention.

---

## 1. Why This Is Not a Convention

### 1.1 Distinguishing Convention from Discovery (Application of the Proposer’s Existing Rule)

The proposer has long maintained the rule: “Not finding a counterexample” does not mean “untrustworthy” — that is, convention and discovery must be kept separate.

If one writes “Every expansion has a starting point, and we call the starting point an anchor,” then the statement is **tautologically true**, because it is naming rather than discovery. A tautology filters out no cases and therefore accomplishes nothing. **Three candidates have already failed for this reason in the current round** (K = Invariant, redundancy = central axis, generativity as an eternal apex).

### 1.2 Why the Present Claim Contains Information

It derives “there must be a limit” from “a token has no spatial definition,” and therefore **predicts an observable consequence**:

> Unrestricted expansion will become unmanageable — we do not know where expansion ends, nor which items should enter.

A prediction can be falsified, so this is a discovery rather than a convention.

### 1.3 Use the Weaker Version

| Version | Statement | Evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| Weak | **We** can only expand from anchors (a limitation of the observer) | **Adopted**. All conclusions today support it, and it does not conflict with STATUS §2.5 (no ontological claim) |
| Strong | Multidimensionality **can only** be expanded from anchors (a claim about multidimensionality itself) | Not adopted. It asserts too much — that no anchorless expansion exists — and requires separate argument |

The weak version is already sufficient to derive all conclusions of this appendix.

## 2. A Constraint Is Not a Decision

### 2.1 Point of Doubt

This round has established that an invariant neither corresponds nor makes decisions. This appendix, however, says that the limit comes from the relation of the topic invariant. **Does providing a limit count as making a decision?**

### 2.2 Ruling

**No. The difference is:**

| | Mode of operation |
|---|---|
| **Constraint** | **Structural** — delineates a range |
| **Decision** | **Item-by-item** — says yes or no about each item within the range |

Analogy: a wall restricts the range in which one can move, but the wall does not inspect who you are.

**This sentence must be fixed**, otherwise “the invariant does not make decisions” will be eroded.

## 3. Expansion Has No End; Construction Expands Only This Far

### 3.1 Statement【Established】

> It is not that further expansion is impossible; **Construction only expands to here**.

### 3.2 This Overturns the Recorder’s Earlier Reading

The recorder originally read “subset” and “substructure” as a **fixed three-level hierarchy** (set → subset → substructure). **Misreading.**

Correct interpretation: **the levels that can be expanded have no end; the three names do not identify three kinds of things, but the depth reached in this expansion.**

### 3.3 Three Consequences

**First, names are relative, not absolute.**  
The substructure in this expansion may become a subset in another expansion, or even an invariant. They are **positions**, not types.

→ **The naming-convergence problem in Appendix B §2.6 is therefore closed**: there is no need to converge on a fixed naming system, because the names move with expansion depth in the first place.

**Second, where one stops is minimality.**  
Expansion stops at the substructure because expanding further contributes nothing to establishment in this case. This is another side of “cannot be further reduced” — **it is not that further expansion is impossible, but that further expansion is unnecessary**.

**Third, the meaning of “minimal” is therefore more precise.**  
Existing documents repeatedly emphasize that “minimal” does not mean minimum data volume, but have not specified what it does mean. Now there is an answer:

> **Minimal = minimum expansion depth.** Expand far enough to be sufficient and then stop; it does not mean retaining the fewest things.

### 3.4 Consequence: The Floor Problem Does Not Hold

If depth is relative, then all formulations that treat the invariant as the “bottom layer” or “floor” must be reconsidered — **it is not the floor; it is the starting point of this expansion**.

Floor: there is nothing below.  
Starting point: there may still be something below; it is simply not unfolded this time.

→ **Appendix B §12.3 (whether the floor should be changed from invariant to relation) is therefore invalid.** The issue is not which thing should be chosen as the floor; the starting point of each expansion is determined by that Construction.

## 4. There Is No Absolute Maximum or Minimum

### 4.1 Statement【Established】

> Every topic invariant in each instance is an anchor, anchored within the multidimensional space. If we force ourselves to deal with maximum and minimum, we can only count more and less upward and downward from the anchor. **Once maximum and minimum values are touched, there is nothing left.**

The key lies in the final sentence: it is not that the value is “uncalculable,” but that **there is nothing at that position**.

### 4.2 This Overturns the Absolute Form of Minimality

```text
∄S′ ⊊ S : Q(T,S′,C,K) = Established
```

This formulation requires checking **all** smaller subsets — a global quantifier that presupposes an external position from which the entire space can be viewed.

**According to §4.1, such a position cannot be occupied: there is no outside.**

### 4.3 Minimality Can Only Be Relative

> Starting **from this anchor**, reducing further downward causes establishment to fail. Change the anchor, and the boundary changes.

**Consistent with “the basis is re-established each time” (Appendix B §10.4).**

It also explains why MCS is non-unique: it is not that “there are multiple solutions”; **each anchor has its own boundary**. This is a fourth source of non-uniqueness, distinct from the previous three (multiple solutions under the same K / changing K changes the MCS / dependence on selection order).

### 4.4 This Explains the Three Collisions in This Round

“How much establishment,” “how much drift,” and “how much added value” — the three questions all stopped at the same place in this round.

**Recorder’s original judgment: a measuring stick is missing.**  
**According to §4.1: it is not a missing scale; those questions have the wrong type.**

They all ask for **absolute values**, while this setting contains only **relative quantities**:

| Question | Is there an answer? |
|---|---|
| How many steps downward from this anchor? | **Yes** |
| How many in total? | **No** — there is nothing at that position |

## 5. Impact on the Formalization Direction

**This is the strongest point of this appendix.**

Original direction: find a definition that can calculate MCS (global).  
**New direction: write relative operations starting from an anchor** — how far upward, how far downward, and where to stop.

**No global structure is required; only a starting point and a direction are required.**

### 5.1 Impact on 《構築_形式化草稿_型別優先》

That draft §4.1 lists two definitions of minimality side by side (M1, global negation; M2, item-by-item removal), and judges that the marginality argument in this round leans toward M2.

**This appendix strengthens that judgment**: M1’s global quantifier cannot be executed when there is no external viewpoint; it is not merely non-equivalent to M2. M2’s item-by-item form, by contrast, only requires comparison relative to the current S and is executable.

**However, M2 still implicitly requires traversing all elements of S**, which is local exhaustiveness rather than global exhaustiveness, and is therefore still feasible.

### 5.2 Not Yet Addressed

The form of relative operations starting from an anchor is **not written in this appendix**. The known constraints are:

- No global quantifier is required
- The starting point is the anchor
- The direction is upward/downward (more/less)
- The stopping condition is “further expansion is unnecessary” (§3.3)
