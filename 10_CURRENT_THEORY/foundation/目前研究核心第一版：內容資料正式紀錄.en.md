# First Version of the Current Research Core
## Shared Structure, Core Invariant Structures, and Incompressible Boundaries

### Version Position

This record is the "First Version of the Current Research Core." It is not intended to preserve the entire research corpus, but to preserve the core structures that can repeatedly be extracted from multiple research topics and that demonstrate cross-topic stability.

Core v1 is not equivalent to a final theory. Its core invariant structures are candidate foundations with high recurrence, cross-topic applicability, and structural necessity in the current research.

# II. Working Definitions of the Core Structures

## 1. Topic

Topic is the primary object and direction around which the current information expansion is organized, allowing different pieces of information to remain identifiable as the same current discussion.

\[
Topic\neq Keyword
\]

Topic also includes its functional position within the current establishment field.

## 2. Invariance

Invariance is the portion of a structure that remains sufficient to preserve the identity of the original structure after the expression, case, or local conditions have changed.

\[
Frequency\neq Invariance
\]

Repeated occurrence does not imply structural invariance.

## 3. Relation

Relation is the connection, dependency, direction, correspondence, comparison, or other structural operation between information units.

\[
Related\neq\text{complete relation description}
\]

Direction, form, and dependency may carry different structural meanings.

## 4. Condition

Condition is the relevant condition required for information, a relation, or a structure to obtain a particular establishment state in the current environment.

\[
Context\neq Validity\ Condition
\]

## 5. Validity

Validity means that a target structure currently has a state sufficient for it to be retained as a valid structure under the relevant conditions.

Provisionally:

\[
Validity(X\mid C)
\]

Validity is not a fixed property independent of conditions.

## 6. Observation

Observation is a phenomenon or piece of information that the system can directly obtain.

\[
Observation\neq Reality
\]

One cannot jump directly from observation to an ontological judgment.

## 7. Interpretation

Interpretation is an acceptable understanding formed from information under the operation of the current context, existing definitions, relations, conditions, and boundaries.

\[
Interpretation
=
F(Observation,Context,Definition,Relation,Boundary)
\]

while maintaining:

\[
Interpretation\neq Observation
\]

\[
Interpretation\neq Reality
\]

## 8. Necessary Structure

The structure that cannot be removed while keeping the target structure established.

\[
Remove(S)\rightarrow Validity\ Loss\ ?
\]

"Important" does not mean "necessary."

## 9. Sufficiency

The state in which information is already sufficient, within the current establishment field, to support the establishment of the current goal.

\[
Sufficient\neq Maximum
\]

Sufficiency must be relative to a Goal or Validity condition.

## 10. Expansion

The process by which an already established structure extends toward relevant subsequent structures.

\[
Current\ Structure\rightarrow New\ Structure
\]

Added information must be traceable to its structural source.

## 11. Anticipation

The directional indication formed by an already established structure toward possible subsequent expansion.

\[
Current\ Structure\rightarrow Possible\ Continuation
\]

Anticipation is not identical to simple prediction.

## 12. Continuation

The portion of necessary relations in an established structure that can be retained into the next round and influence the formation of the next-round structure.

\[
Structure_t\rightarrow Structure_{t+1}
\]

## 13. State

State represents the current research / establishment position of a piece of information or definition, such as Established, Candidate, Working, or Unverified.

\[
Candidate\neq Definition
\]

State itself is part of the research information.

## 14. Boundary

Boundary describes how far an information item, structure, or judgment may extend under the current establishment conditions, and when it begins to leave the current valid range.

A boundary is not simply a maximum value; it is the structural transition of the currently valid range.

# IV. The Most Important Property of Core v1

The strongest common feature is not that "they are all important," but that:

> **They are repeatedly reused across different problems, and each reuse retains certain common structural functions.**

Therefore, the current core definition is not captured by textual frequency, but by:

\[
Cross\text{-}Interpretive\ Structural\ Necessity
\]

That is, whether a structure crosses multiple interpretive units while continuously performing an irreplaceable structural function within them.

---

# V. Core v1 Incompressibility Principle

\[
\boxed{
Compression\ cannot\ remove\ necessary\ relations.
}
\]

> **Information can be shortened, but relations necessary for maintaining establishment cannot be eliminated merely because the representation is shortened.**

At the same time:

\[
\boxed{
Repeated\ structure\ cannot\ be\ assumed\ redundant.
}
\]

A repeatedly occurring structure still needs to be classified as Redundancy, Invariant, Context Dependency, or Continuation Requirement rather than being assumed redundant.
