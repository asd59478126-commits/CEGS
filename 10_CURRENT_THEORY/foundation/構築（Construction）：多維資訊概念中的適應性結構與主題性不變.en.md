# Construction
## Adaptive Structure and Thematic Invariance in the Concept of Multidimensional Information

> This document is the theory update dated 2026-09-12. The original date record is retained in `2026.9.10/`; the new derivations from this update are additionally retained in `2026.9.12/`.

### Abstract

“Construction” is a research program concerning how multidimensional information, concepts, and structures are organized, understood, adapted, and maintained as valid.

This research does not take the establishment of a unique conceptual form, a unique methodology, or a unique minimal structure as its ultimate purpose. When the object of study has multiple information dimensions, different task requirements, and different conditions of use, what needs to be maintained may not be surface form, but thematic and structural continuity under task changes.

> **Construction can adjust its structural form according to task purpose, task goal, and conditions of validity while maintaining its thematic invariance.**

Therefore, different Construction methods may be valid simultaneously and need not be compressed into a single method. Construction is currently positioned as an operational, analyzable, and extensible theoretical concept; it does not claim uniqueness, ultimate truth, or a fixed ontological status.

---

# I. Research Question

Construction is not simply information compression, summarization, or classification. It studies:

> **When information or a concept with multiple dimensions cannot be fully expanded, what structure must be retained so that the object of understanding can remain established?**

Core questions include which information is merely surface expression, which relations perform necessary functions, which structures can be replaced, which cannot be removed, and how a structure can retain its original topic after the task changes.

# III. Construction and Minimality

Early research proposed the “Minimum Construction Structure (MCS)”: if removing a structure causes the target to no longer remain established under the original conditions, that structure is necessary.

Subsequent research found that:

\[
MCS_1(E)\neq MCS_2(E)
\]

may still both make \(E\) established. Therefore, minimality may depend on task purpose, task goal, conditions of validity, the comparison scale, and the Construction method.

The current research does not treat a unique MCS as a necessary condition for Construction to be established.

> **MCS is a structure that cannot be further simplified while still maintaining the target as established under specified tasks, conditions, and comparison scales.**

# VIII. Mapping Layers of State, Representation, and Interpretation

Construction should not understand information transmission as transparent transfer.

The internal state of a subject, after an act of expression, forms a representation that can be obtained externally; the receiver then forms its own understanding based on that representation.

It can therefore be represented as:

\[
S_0
\rightarrow
R_1
\rightarrow
I_2
\]

where:

- \(S_0\): the subject’s internal state.
- \(R_1\): the representation/record formed after the subject externalizes the state.
- \(I_2\): the understanding formed by another subject based on the representation.

The three should not be directly treated as the same object:

\[
S_0\neq R_1
\]

\[
R_1\neq I_2
\]

and therefore one cannot directly assume:

\[
S_0=I_2
\]

An externalized representation is not mere transmission, but a structural formation in which a subject converts an internal state into a form that can be obtained externally.

Likewise, understanding is not a direct copy of a representation, but a re-formation of structure by the receiver based on the external representation.

Therefore, Construction can occur at least in two adjacent mappings:

\[
State\rightarrow Representation
\]

and:

\[
Representation\rightarrow Interpretation
\]

The former concerns how a subject constructs a state into obtainable information; the latter concerns how another subject forms its own understanding from information.

If there is no completely transparent transmission of information across subjects, then information continuity should not be required to rest on complete identity of form.

Even if:

\[
S_0\neq R_1
\]

and:

\[
R_1\neq I_2
\]

it may still be possible, under specific conditions, to maintain traceable thematic continuity.

This may temporarily be represented as:

\[
Theme(S_0)\approx Theme(R_1)\approx Theme(I_2)
\]

Here \(\approx\) is not currently specified as a strict mathematical equivalence relation; it only denotes a candidate relation in which “thematic continuity remains traceable.”

This section further retains the non-compressible boundaries:

\[
Internal\ State\neq External\ Representation
\]

\[
External\ Representation\neq Interpretation
\]

The purpose is not to claim that information must necessarily become distorted, but to prohibit different stages of formation from being mistaken for the same information object.

For AI interaction, AI cannot directly obtain another subject’s first-person internal state from a textual representation. What AI can obtain is an externalized information structure available for reading, from which it forms its own understanding or inference.

Therefore:

> AI’s understanding of a person is first an understanding of the externalized representation, not direct access to the original internal state.

This is not an AI-specific defect; it is first of all a general structural condition of cross-subject information formation.

This section is currently positioned as a supplementary theoretical structure. The precise criterion for “thematic continuity,” the formalization of the three-layer mapping, and which changes constitute thematic drift remain subjects for further research.

Construction can currently answer:

1. How is it used?
2. How can it be applied?
3. How difficult is it to use?

The current researchers judge that Construction has reached a usable degree of working completeness, while retaining room for external validation and further research.

---

# XIV. What This Version Does Not Claim

This version does not claim: a unique MCS, a unique form, a philosophical school, a complete description of the whole human mind, a specific neural mechanism, or the replacement of existing academic methods.

---

# XV. Current Research Conclusion

> **Construction is a theoretical concept for studying how multidimensional information structures remain usable and adaptable, while maintaining thematic continuity, under different task purposes and task goals.**

Its core can be condensed as:

\[
\boxed{
Purpose+Goal+Conditions\rightarrow Adaptive\ Construction
}
\]

while maintaining:

\[
\boxed{
Thematic\ Invariance
}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Adaptive Form}\neq\text{Invariant Form}
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\text{Multiple Valid Constructions}\neq\text{Theoretical Failure}
}
\]

Subsequent questions need not presuppose that uniqueness must be proven. Research can instead continue on the conditions under which multiple Constructions can remain valid simultaneously, what truly remains invariant thematically across different Constructions, and how far the adaptability of Construction can be extended without losing the conditions of validity.
