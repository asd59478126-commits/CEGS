# Foundational Assumptions of the Exchange Principle: Information Difference, Need, and Exchange Formation

## I. Research Position

This document records the first-layer foundational assumptions, structures, and examples currently developed for the "Exchange Principle."

This stage does not claim to establish a complete exchange-governance model. It first describes:

> **Why exchange arises, and what basic structural conditions exist before exchange can be established.**

The current working structure is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Information / Cognitive Difference}
\rightarrow
\text{Need for Communication}
\rightarrow
\text{Need for Exchange}
\rightarrow
\text{Exchange}
}
\]

as the first-layer working structure.

---

## II. Foundational Assumption

Exchange does not occur unconditionally.

Its emergence can provisionally be understood as:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Difference}
\rightarrow
\text{Need for Communication}
\rightarrow
\text{Need for Exchange}
}
\]

That is, different entities may have some difference that cannot be eliminated directly by themselves, generating a need for communication and exchange.

Here, "difference" is not limited to a simple data difference. It may include:

\[
\text{Information Difference}
\]

\[
\text{State Difference}
\]

\[
\text{Capability Difference}
\]

\[
\text{Perspective Difference}
\]

\[
\text{Requirement Difference}
\]

as well as other structural differences not yet fully classified.

---

## III. Information Difference and Cognitive Difference

Two major information levels are provisionally distinguished:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Physical Information}
}
\]

and:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Cognitive Information}
}
\]

Physical information can represent the actual environment, objects, states, and observable conditions.

Cognitive information concerns:

- what an individual knows;
- what an individual does not know;
- what an individual wants;
- how an individual understands the current situation;
- what judgment an individual makes about exchange conditions.

Therefore:

\[
\text{Physical State}
\neq
\text{Cognitive State}
\]

but the two may jointly constitute exchange conditions.

---

## IV. The Starting Point of Exchange Is Not an Information Object, but Difference and Need

The current framework does not treat:

\[
\text{Information Object}
\]

as the ultimate starting cause of exchange.

A more suitable working representation is:

\[
\text{Difference}
\rightarrow
\text{Need}
\rightarrow
\text{Exchange}
\]

That is:

> **Exchange does not occur merely because information exists. Rather, information exchange becomes one possible necessary means because different individuals have differences that need to be addressed.**

---

## V. Formation of Need

Need may first be represented as:

\[
\text{Current Need}
\]

A need does not necessarily require complex cognitive reasoning.

For example:

\[
\text{"Want a coffee"}
\]

can itself form a need for exchange.

Its basic chain can be represented as:

\[
\text{Need}
\rightarrow
\text{Object / Service}
\rightarrow
\text{Potential Exchange}
\]

Therefore, exchange governance should not assume that all exchanges originate in complex information processing.

Some exchanges may require only very basic need formation.

---

## VI. Example: Buying a Cup of Coffee

"Buying coffee" can serve as a foundational example for the Exchange Principle.

Its most simplified structure is:

\[
\text{Need: Want a coffee}
\]

which further forms:

\[
\text{Coffee}
\rightarrow
\text{Object of Exchange}
\]

But whether exchange is established also depends on other information:

\[
\text{Price}
\]

\[
\text{Available Resource}
\]

\[
\text{Current Need}
\]

and related factors.

Therefore:

\[
\text{Need}
\neq
\text{Exchange}
\]

Need only creates the possibility of exchange.

---

## VII. Demand Peak

In the coffee example, if:

\[
\text{Price}
>
\text{Acceptable Range}
\]

then:

\[
\text{Need}
\]

may not be transformed into:

\[
\text{Exchange}
\]

Therefore the following working concept is proposed:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Demand Peak}
}
\]

"Demand Peak" provisionally means:

> **The boundary of conditions under which a need is willing to support the establishment of an exchange under the current situation and conditions.**

It can therefore provisionally be represented as:

\[
\text{Current Need}
+
\text{Conditions}
\rightarrow
\text{Demand Peak}
\]

followed by:

\[
\text{Demand Peak}
\rightarrow
\text{Exchange Decision}
\]

---

## VIII. Demand Peak Is Not a Fixed Value

Demand Peak is not assumed to be permanently fixed.

It may be affected by:

\[
\text{Current Context}
\]

\[
\text{Task}
\]

\[
\text{Resource State}
\]

and possibly:

\[
\text{Memory}
\]

\[
\text{Emotion}
\]

Therefore:

\[
DP_t
\neq
DP_{t+1}
\]

This does not mean that the need itself has disappeared; it may mean that the current establishment conditions have changed.

---

## IX. Upstream Effects of Memory and Emotion

Based on the previous Memory Equivalent research, the following can provisionally be considered:

\[
\text{Memory Equivalent}
\rightarrow
\text{Current Relevance / Need}
\]

Emotion may affect:

\[
\text{Emotion State}
\rightarrow
\text{Memory Retrieval Priority}
\]

thereby forming:

\[
\text{Emotion}
\rightarrow
\text{Memory Re-entry}
\rightarrow
\text{Current Need}
\]

Thus, the following extension chain can currently be proposed but remains to be validated:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Memory / Emotion}
\rightarrow
\text{Current Need}
\rightarrow
\text{Demand Peak}
\rightarrow
\text{Exchange}
}
\]

This is currently only a conceptual connection and is not claimed to constitute a complete causal model.

---

## X. First-Layer Multidimensional Structure of Exchange Governance

The first layer can provisionally be condensed as:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Physical / Cognitive Difference}
\rightarrow
\text{Communication Need}
\rightarrow
\text{Exchange Need}
\rightarrow
\text{Demand Boundary}
\rightarrow
\text{Exchange}
}
\]

Each layer may itself expand into a multidimensional structure.

Therefore, exchange governance is not simply:

\[
\text{Information}
\rightarrow
\text{Transfer}
\]

but is closer to:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Difference Resolution through Exchange}
}
\]

---

## XI. Questions Not Yet Addressed

This stage does not yet attempt to handle:

- complete exchange qualification;
- third-party exchange;
- allocation of authority and responsibility;
- post-exchange state governance;
- recursion across repeated exchanges;
- large-scale exchange fields;
- exchange conflicts;
- a complete supply-and-demand model.

These belong to later levels of exchange governance.

For now, the first layer is preserved as:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Difference}
\rightarrow
\text{Need}
\rightarrow
\text{Exchange}
}
\]

and:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Demand Peak}
}
\]

as an initial boundary concept between need and exchange.

---

## XIII. Most Simplified Representation

The first layer of the "Exchange Principle" can currently be condensed into:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Information / Cognitive Difference}
\rightarrow
\text{Need for Communication}
\rightarrow
\text{Need for Exchange}
\rightarrow
\text{Demand Peak}
\rightarrow
\text{Exchange Decision}
}
\]

where:

\[
\text{Difference}
\]

is the upstream condition for exchange;

\[
\text{Need}
\]

is the direct need that produces exchange;

\[
\text{Demand Peak}
\]

is the initial boundary determining whether need can become exchange;

and:

\[
\text{Exchange}
\]

is the final behavior.

The current research stops at this layer.
