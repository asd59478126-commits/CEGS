# Construction: Supplement on the Mapping of State, Representation, and Interpretation

> Date: 2026-09-12
> Status: Theoretical supplement / candidate structure; not yet claimed as a final definition.
> Source: Derived from the chain "Internal State of a Subject → Externalized Representation → Understanding by Another Subject."

## I. Core Observation

Information transmission in Construction should not be understood as transparent transportation.

A subject's internal state is formed into a representation that can be obtained externally through an act of expression; the recipient then forms its own understanding from that representation.

It can therefore be represented as:

\[
S_0
\rightarrow
R_1
\rightarrow
I_2
\]

where:

- \(S_0\): the subject's internal state.
- \(R_1\): the representation / record formed after externalization by the subject.
- \(I_2\): the understanding formed by another subject from the representation.

The three should not be directly treated as the same object:

\[
S_0\neq R_1
\]

\[
R_1\neq I_2
\]

and therefore one cannot directly assume:

\[
S_0=I_2
\]

## II. The Mapping Itself Is Also Construction

Externalized representation is not mere transmission. It is one structural formation in which a subject converts an internal state into a form that can be obtained externally.

Likewise, understanding is not a direct copy of the representation. It is a new structure formed by the recipient on the basis of the external representation.

Therefore Construction can occur in at least two adjacent mappings:

\[
State\rightarrow Representation
\]

and:

\[
Representation\rightarrow Interpretation
\]

The former concerns how a subject constructs an attainable information form from a state; the latter concerns how another subject forms its own understanding from information.

## III. Relation to Thematic Invariance

If cross-subject information transmission is not completely transparent, information continuity should not be required to rest on forms being exactly identical.

Even when:

\[
S_0\neq R_1
\]

and:

\[
R_1\neq I_2
\]

traceable thematic continuity may still be maintained under particular conditions.

It can therefore provisionally be represented as:

\[
Theme(S_0)\approx Theme(R_1)\approx Theme(I_2)
\]

Here \(\approx\) is not yet assigned the meaning of a strict mathematical equivalence relation; it only denotes a candidate relation in which "the theme remains traceable."

## IV. Direct Non-Identity

This supplement further preserves and expands an existing incompressible boundary:

\[
Observation\neq Reality
\]

\[
Representation\neq Reality
\]

and introduces the following candidate boundaries:

\[
Internal\ State\neq External\ Representation
\]

\[
External\ Representation\neq Interpretation
\]

The purpose is not to claim that information must be distorted, but to prohibit the different stages of formation from being mistaken for the same information object.

## V. Direct Implication for AI Interaction

AI cannot directly obtain another subject's first-person internal state from a textual representation.

What AI can obtain is an already externalized information structure available for reading, from which it forms its own understanding or inference.

Therefore:

> AI's understanding of a person is first an understanding of an externalized representation, not direct access to the person's original internal state.

This is not a defect unique to AI; it is first of all a general structural condition of cross-subject information formation.

## VI. Research Status

The supplement can currently be included as an additional structure in Construction theory, but the following remain open research questions:

1. A precise criterion for "thematic continuity."
2. Whether stricter formal relations can be established among \(S_0\), \(R_1\), and \(I_2\).
3. Under what conditions a representation is sufficient to support stable cross-subject understanding.
4. Which changes during a mapping process count as adaptation and which constitute thematic drift.
